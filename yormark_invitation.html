I · The Letter

An invitation, not a proposal.

Michael, Dana and Sophia, this note is exactly what it appears to be. A personal invitation to spend three nights with us at Thanda, in northern KwaZulu-Natal, between 16 and 19 May.

There is no agenda set up around your visit. No presentation in the diary. No business arranged on the back of your stay. The only ask is that you arrive, settle in, and let the place do what it does.

Thanda sits within 16,500 hectares of Big Five reserve, an hour by helicopter from King Shaka International Airport in Durban. The Olofsson family of Malmö founded it 25 years ago and have never marketed it. There has been no campaign, no press tour, no public launch. Seven of the 44 Royal Residences have been built. Each one sold quietly, by introduction.

We are inviting you because the people we know who have spent time here have, almost without exception, said the same thing afterwards. That something shifted. That is hard to write in a brochure, which is partly why we do not write brochures.

The dates are set to follow the Forbes Global Properties launch on 15 May in Cape Town, so the logistics work either way you arrive. If the timing does not fit, tell us what does and we will move it.

For Sophia, the reserve has things she will remember for the rest of her life. The Junior Rangers Course, photography afternoons with our resident wildlife photographer, bush picnics in places children do not normally get to eat, and a night under an outdoor cinema lit only by a Marula tree. For all three of you, the days are slow, the food is exceptional, and the silence is the kind you do not get anywhere else.

We hope you come.

II · The Place

Thanda means love in Zulu.

A 16,500 hectare Big Five reserve in northern KwaZulu-Natal, on land that contains some of the oldest carbon-dated records of human presence in Africa.

The Ingwavuma Border Cave, on Thanda's eastern edge, has yielded human artefacts dated back roughly 200,000 years. The land itself has been continuously inhabited and lived with for that long. It is, in a real sense, ancestral country.

Christin and Dan Olofsson, of Malmö in Sweden, founded Thanda in 2001 and have invested over 100 million US dollars into the land, the wildlife, and the surrounding communities. Their philanthropic arm, Star for Life, has supported more than 500,000 young people across Southern Africa since 2005. Princess Nandi Zulu serves as the reserve's brand ambassador, a relationship blessed by the late King Goodwill Zwelithini ka Bhekuzulu.

Thanda is one of three South African safari operations admitted to Leading Hotels of the World, and was named World's Leading Luxury Exclusive Private Villa in Safari at the 2024 World Travel Awards. The Getty family own the neighbouring estate.

III · The Royal Residences

Forty-four homes. And not one more.

Within the reserve sits a 386 hectare gated estate. It holds 44 freehold Royal Residences. Membership of The Royal Thanda Club is conditional on ownership.

Four villa models are available: Induna, Inkosi, Isilo, and Indlovukazi, each in either Thanda Traditional or Thanda Contemporary architecture. Sizes range from 759 to 1,862 square metres of covered area, with four to six bedrooms. Pricing falls between 6.3 and 10.9 million US dollars, all-inclusive of erf, villa, and full membership.

Every Royal Residence includes a private helicopter safari service. To our knowledge, no other property in Africa offers this. Each title deed includes 80 years of traversing rights over the adjoining 6,700 hectare Mduna Royal Reserve, through a long-standing lease with the Ingonyama Trust Board. Members and their families have preferred access to Thanda Island, the Olofsson family's private island off the Tanzanian coast.

We do not show this part of Thanda lightly. If, by Sunday evening of your stay, you would like a quiet conversation about what membership looks like, we will have one. If not, we will not raise it. The visit is not contingent on the conversation.

IV · The Family Stay

Designed around the three of you.

The pace is slow. The land is enormous. The detail is in the small things.

For Sophia

The Junior Rangers Course is run by Thanda's senior tracking team. Sophia will spend a morning identifying spoor, plant life, and birdsong with the same instructors who train our guides. Christian Sperka, Thanda's resident wildlife photographer, will spend an afternoon with her on a real tracking session, teaching her to use a camera in the bush.

There is an outdoor cinema set up beneath the sacred Marula tree, and a helicopter day to Nine Mile Beach inside iSimangaliso, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, where the dolphins come close enough to the shore to watch from the sand.

For Michael & Dana

Twice-daily Big Five game drives with private guides. Bush breakfasts in places you would not expect to eat. A traditional Zulu cultural ceremony hosted by a sangoma from the neighbouring community. Dinner at the Marula Tree, prepared by Thanda's executive chef.

The Himalayan salt sauna and yoga deck. Stargazing with no ambient light for a hundred kilometres in any direction. If there is rhino or cheetah work being done by our conservation team during your stay, you can ride along.

If You Want Something Else

Helicopter to Prince's Grant or Zimbali for a round of golf. Horseback safari through the bush. Diving the iSimangaliso reef, which holds over 1,250 marine species. Leatherback turtle tracking by night, in season. Tell us in advance and we will arrange it.
