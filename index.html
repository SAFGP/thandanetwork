Zululand, untouched→

02 · The land

Thanda lies in the ancient heart of Zululand, a landscape that has shaped Southern African history for six hundred years. It is the homeland of King Shaka, a place of legend, lineage and living wildlife. The reserve spans 16,500 hectares of savannah, acacia woodland and wetland, traversed by the Big Five and more than four hundred bird species.

Within it, a 386 hectare private estate has been set aside exclusively for The Royal Thanda Club, wrapped inside a sanctuary the size of a small country. Every residence looks out onto unbroken bush. There are no neighbours to see. There are only the animals, the thornveld, and the silence.

Built to be stewarded→

04 · The ethos

Thanda was never intended to grow quickly. Pierre Delvaux built it as a generational project, a promise to return the land to its natural state and keep it that way for the families who choose to be part of it. Every decision, from architecture to membership, has been filtered through that promise. Ownership here is not a transaction. It is an invitation into a place of peace, nature, and memory that endures across generations.

  1. 01

    Conservation as default

    A portion of every sale funds rhino anti-poaching, cheetah rehabilitation, and community programmes across the Mduna Royal Reserve. Ownership participates in an active conservation economy.

  2. 02

    Architectural palette

    Every residence draws from natural elements: stone, thatch, timber. The design language is curated yet flexible, allowing individual expression within a cohesive aesthetic inspired by the landscape itself.

  3. 03

    Absolute privacy

    No public lodge. No day trippers. No signage at the gate. Members and their guests are the only people who ever see the interior of The Royal Thanda Club.

The Residences→

05 · Fifteen remain

Multiple villa options across different styles and opportunities, each set within protected bushveld corridors. Every residence is delivered fully turnkey, fully furnished, and fully staffed from the day of handover.

The architectural language is quiet and deliberate. Natural stone walls, hand thatched roofs, oiled timber, unfinished brass, linen in warm earthen tones. The interiors were developed in collaboration with South African ateliers and anchored in the materials of the land itself.
